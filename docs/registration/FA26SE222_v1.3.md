**3. Register content of Capstone Project**

**(\*) 3.1. Capstone Project name:**

3.1.1. English: ***LuxMap: A GIS, IoT and Computer Vision Platform for Rural Road Lighting Asset and Fault Management***

3.1.2. Vietnamese: ***LuxMap: Hệ thống bản đồ số GIS tích hợp IoT và thị giác máy tính để quản lý tài sản và sự cố chiếu sáng đường giao thông nông thôn***

**Abbreviation: LuxMap**

**(\*) 3.2. Main proposal content (including result and product)**

**a) Context:**

Rural road lighting in Vietnam has expanded rapidly under the new-rural-development program, but installation has outpaced management. Communes rarely hold an accurate fixture inventory, faults are usually found only when a resident complains (often weeks later), and a fixture that still turns on but gives much less light is classified as working by an on/off inspection.

Fitting an IoT node to every pole is unaffordable, shared electrical causes such as a tripped breaker are logged as separate lamp faults, and repairs are not prioritized by the importance of the location (a school approach or a bridge). This project gives communes a low-cost way to know where their lights are, which have failed or dimmed, and which repairs matter most.

**b) Proposed Solutions:**

- **GIS asset register:** maps poles, fixtures, feeders and road segments (with importance levels) as the common database.
- **Night survey with AI:** Field Engineers survey poles with a smartphone and a light sensor; an AI model detects ON/OFF, and each pole’s reading, compared with its own baseline, gives Normal / Dim / Out.
- **Sparse IoT monitoring:** nodes on selected assets monitor electrical conditions and support remote ON/OFF/AUTO control; feeder links help find shared causes.
- **Citizen reporting:** residents report problems through the QR code on each pole.
- **Fault and maintenance management:** problems are shown on the GIS map, ranked by severity and location importance, assigned as tasks and tracked to completion.

**c) Functional Requirements:**

- **Superior:** (1) view network status, fixture conditions and fault statistics on the GIS map; (2) view and export analytical and maintenance reports.
- **Manager (Web Application):** (1) manage poles, feeders, road segments (with importance level) and maintenance history; (2) create and assign survey routes and review results, including Dim and Out fixtures and segments with consecutive failed poles; (3) review faults reported by citizens, field engineers, and IoT devices; group faults along the same power feeder to identify common causes; and create, assign, and prioritize inspection and repair tasks by severity and location importance; (4) monitor and control (ON, OFF, AUTO) supported streetlights; (5) manage Field Engineers’ schedules and task status.
- **Field Engineer (Mobile Application):** (1) follow survey routes, capture each pole’s fixture and collect its light reading through the paired light-sensing module, offline with later synchronization; (2) submit survey sessions, view results and re-survey rejected segments; (3) view assigned tasks and navigate to the asset; (4) verify detected conditions, report faults and upload evidence; (5) update task status and submit inspection and repair reports.
- **System Admin:** (1) manage user accounts, roles and permissions; (2) manage system configurations; (3) monitor system operation and view system logs.
- **Citizen:** (1) scan the QR code on a registered light pole; (2) submit a lighting-issue report, optionally with photos.

**d) Non-Functional Requirements:**

- **Spatial Data Quality:** every pole, fixture and road segment has valid coordinates (within 5 m of the field position), and at least 95% of survey observations and sensor readings are linked to the correct registered pole; wrong locations are corrected by authorized users and wrong links by re-survey.
- **Recognition and Classification Accuracy:** ON/OFF reaches an F1-score of at least 0.85 on a held-out test set (precision and recall reported per class); Normal / Dim / Out agree with field verification for at least 85% of surveyed poles (a reference lux meter on a subset of poles and a filter of known light reduction).
- **Measurement Consistency:** surveys follow a documented protocol, and per-pole readings across repeated surveys under the same protocol have a coefficient of variation of at most 10%.
- **Processing Turnaround:** results of a survey session of up to 100 poles are available to the Manager within 5 minutes of submission, measured on pilot sessions.
- **Offline Tolerance and Affordability:** IoT devices and the Mobile Application buffer data for at least 24 hours of network loss with no record lost after synchronization; the design uses smartphone surveys, a low-cost light sensor and few IoT devices.
- **Usability and Auditability:** commune staff can operate the map, fault and work-order screens without GIS or machine-learning knowledge (at least 90% of walkthrough tasks completed without assistance by at least 3 test users); records follow the data-retention policy, with an audit trail of key automated findings and user actions.

**e) Theory & Practical:**

**Theory**

The project applies geographic information systems (spatial modeling of poles, feeders and road segments), photometry (illuminance, the inverse-square and cosine laws, and why readings are compared per pole under a consistent protocol), night-time computer vision (ON/OFF recognition under glare, with smartphone exposure control), baseline-based degradation detection (each pole against its own history) and electrical condition monitoring (current and state, with feeder topology for shared causes).

**Practical**

In a pilot area, the team will (1) build the GIS register of poles, fixtures, road segments and feeders; (2) assemble a light-sensing module and a limited IoT prototype; (3) define a survey protocol and build an annotated ON/OFF dataset; (4) train and evaluate the ON/OFF recognition model; (5) build the Web GIS, offline Mobile Application and maintenance workflow; and (6) evaluate against field verification. Where field data or hardware are limited, recorded survey data and simulated dimming with a filter of known light reduction serve as fallback.

**f) Products (Expected Deliverables):**

- **Web GIS Platform and Backend:** source code and a deployed demo for asset management, fixture conditions, IoT information, faults (including Citizen QR reports), repair prioritization and assignment, and remote lighting control, with role-based access and audit logging.
- **Mobile Field Module:** an offline-capable mobile application for survey routes, smartphone capture, light-module pairing, survey submission, tasks and fault reporting.
- **Hardware Prototypes and Firmware:** a survey light-sensing module and an IoT electrical-monitoring prototype with abnormal-condition detection and data buffering.
- **Annotated Fixture Dataset and AI Model:** labeled ON/OFF fixtures with pole identifiers, timestamps and light readings; the trained ON/OFF recognition model; and the survey procedure.
- **Documentation and Evaluation:** test report, user and deployment documents, and a field trial report (ON/OFF performance, agreement of Normal / Dim / Out with field verification, measurement repeatability, IoT results and processing performance).

**g) Proposed Tasks:**

- **Member 1 – Project Manager / Backend Developer (TP1):** Plan and track the project; analyze requirements and design the architecture; implement backend APIs for authentication, roles, fault management (including Citizen QR reports) and work orders; integrate and deploy; run the pilot field evaluation and write the field trial and test reports. Output: architecture document, core backend APIs, deployed system, field trial report, test report.
- **Member 2 – Backend / IoT Developer (TP2):** Implement backend services for assets and IoT devices (registration, telemetry, power state and current monitoring, ON/OFF/AUTO control); build the IoT monitoring prototype and the light-sensing module with firmware; evaluate the IoT prototype in the pilot. Output: IoT services and prototype, firmware, test logs, IoT evaluation results.
- **Member 3 – Web / GIS Frontend Developer (TP3):** Build the Web GIS application with lighting map, asset and road-segment management, dashboard and reports, and fault, work-order and scheduling interfaces; coordinate field surveys with the commune; evaluate the Web application in the pilot. Output: Web application, survey coordination plan, Web evaluation results.
- **Member 4 – Mobile Developer / Field Operations (TP4):** Build the offline Mobile Application with task and schedule views, navigation, survey capture and synchronization, sensor pairing, fault reporting and evidence upload; evaluate the Mobile Application in the pilot. Output: mobile application, test cases, mobile evaluation results.
- **Member 5 – AI / Computer Vision Developer (TP5):** Annotate the night-lighting dataset; train and evaluate ON/OFF recognition; associate fixtures with poles; derive Normal / Dim / Out conditions from ON/OFF results and per-pole baselines; evaluate the ON/OFF model and the Normal / Dim / Out agreement against field verification. Output: annotated dataset, trained model, evaluation log.

**4. Other comments (propose all relative things if have):**

A sparse set of IoT devices and periodic smartphone surveys keep deployment cost low; light-sensor readings are used for relative comparison of the same pole across surveys, not as certified absolute illuminance. Field validation depends on night-survey locations with fixtures that are ON, OFF and visibly dimmed, surveyed repeatedly; software development can proceed independently, with recorded survey data and simulated dimming as fallback.
