3.1.1. English: ***LuxMap: A GIS, IoT and Computer Vision Platform for Rural Road Lighting Asset and Fault Management***

3.1.2. Vietnamese: ***LuxMap: Hệ thống bản đồ số GIS tích hợp IoT và thị giác máy tính để quản lý tài sản và sự cố chiếu sáng đường giao thông nông thôn***

**Abbreviation: LuxMap**

**(\*) 3.2. Main proposal content (including result and product)**

**a) Context:**

Rural road lighting in Vietnam has expanded rapidly under the new-rural-development programme, with communes installing large numbers of grid-connected street lights along inter-commune and inter-village roads. Installation, however, has outpaced management. A commune rarely holds an accurate inventory of what was installed, where, of what type, or when its warranty expires, and a fault is normally discovered only when a resident complains — which on a low-traffic rural road can take weeks. The problem is compounded by the difficulty of detecting gradual lighting degradation: a fixture may still turn on but provide significantly lower illumination than expected, so a simple on/off inspection can classify it as working even when lighting quality has deteriorated.

- **No reliable asset inventory:** Communes cannot state how many fixtures exist, their location, type, power source or warranty status, so maintenance cannot be planned or budgeted.

- **Faults are found by complaint, not by inspection:** Discovery depends on a resident reporting a dark stretch, which is slow and biased toward the busiest roads.

- Degradation is invisible to on/off monitoring: A dimming lamp may still register as working in a binary check even though its illumination has deteriorated and the road is inadequately lit.

- **Instrumenting every pole is unaffordable:** A district may manage thousands of fixtures, so fitting an IoT node to each one exceeds any rural maintenance budget.

- **Segment-level causes are misdiagnosed as lamp faults:** A tripped breaker, failed timer or stolen cable darkens a whole stretch, but without spatial reasoning each dark pole is logged and dispatched separately.

- **Repairs are unprioritized:** Crews are mobilized one complaint at a time, with no basis for treating an unlit school approach or bridge as more urgent than a field road.

**b) Proposed Solutions:**

Build LuxMap, a GIS platform in which lighting assets and road segments are mapped and monitored through two complementary sensing channels designed for a rural budget. A sparse set of IoT nodes monitors the electrical operating conditions of selected lighting assets, including power state and current consumption. Periodic night surveys are conducted from a moving vehicle carrying a smartphone, whose rear camera is angled obliquely upward toward the roadside fixtures so that a single fixture is captured clearly as the vehicle passes its pole, together with a light-sensing module based on the BH1750FVI digital light sensor that records timestamped illuminance readings. A computer vision model determines whether each passed fixture is ON or OFF, while the illuminance peak recorded as the vehicle passes the pole provides a per-pole light measurement. Using smartphone GPS, timestamps and the pole positions registered in the GIS, each observation is associated with its pole, and the ON/OFF result is combined with the per-pole light measurement to determine the fixture condition (Normal, Dim or Out). The results are displayed on the GIS map to support lighting inspection, fault identification, and maintenance activities.

- **GIS Lighting Asset and Road Segment Register:** Maps and manages lighting poles, fixtures, and road segments together with relevant information such as lamp specifications, installation date, warranty, location, and maintenance history. Existing asset data can be imported into the system when available, while missing or newly installed assets can be added and updated manually through the GIS interface.

- **Sparse IoT Electrical Monitoring:** IoT nodes installed on selected lighting assets monitor electrical operating conditions such as power state and current consumption to support basic detection of abnormal operating conditions without requiring a monitoring node on every pole.

- **Night Survey Capture:** A smartphone mounted on a moving vehicle captures continuous night video, with the rear camera angled obliquely upward toward the roadside fixtures, while recording GPS location, heading and timestamps. A fixed mounting position, camera angle and locked exposure settings are used so that fixtures are not saturated in the image and results remain comparable between survey sessions.

- **Fixture ON/OFF Recognition:** Video frames around each pole passing are processed by a computer vision model that detects the lighting fixture in the frame and classifies it as ON or OFF, distinguishing the fixture from other light sources such as vehicle headlights, shop signs and house lights, while considering challenges such as glare, bloom and motion blur.

- **Per-Pole Light Measurement:** A light-sensing module based on the BH1750FVI digital light sensor and a microcontroller is mounted facing upward on the survey vehicle and samples illuminance continuously during night surveys. The peak reading recorded as the vehicle passes each pole is taken as that pole's light measurement for the survey session.

- **Survey Data Association:** Video frames, ON/OFF results, light-sensor readings and smartphone GPS data are synchronized using timestamps and associated with the corresponding lighting pole in the GIS, using the pole positions already registered in the system together with the time of the illuminance peak to handle GPS inaccuracy, poles on both sides of the road, and intersections.

- **Fixture Condition Determination:** The ON/OFF result and the per-pole light measurement are combined to classify each fixture. A fixture recognized as OFF is classified as Out; a fixture recognized as ON whose light measurement falls below a configurable proportion of that pole's own baseline from earlier surveys is classified as Dim; otherwise it is Normal. The lighting status of each road segment is derived from the condition of the poles along it, for example by flagging segments with consecutive Out or Dim poles.

- **Operating Condition Analysis:** Electrical operating state and current consumption from selected IoT-monitored lighting assets are analyzed using predefined rules to identify basic abnormal conditions such as power loss, unexpected OFF states or abnormal current consumption.

- **Lighting Fault and Maintenance Management:** Fixtures classified as Dim or Out, road segments with consecutive failed poles, and lighting assets with abnormal electrical operating conditions are displayed on the GIS map. Managers can review detected problems, create inspection or repair tasks, assign them to Field Engineers, and track maintenance activities until completion.

**d) Non-Functional Requirements:**

- **Geospatial Data and Association Accuracy:** Lighting assets and road segments must contain valid geographic information for GIS visualization. Survey observations, ON/OFF results, and light-sensor measurements must be associated with the correct lighting pole using smartphone GPS data, timestamps, and registered pole positions, and the association accuracy must be evaluated during the pilot. Authorized users must be able to review and correct inaccurate asset locations and pole associations.

- **Feeder Topology Coverage:** Lighting poles should be associated with their corresponding feeder or electrical supply group where such information is available. This topology information is displayed in the GIS and used to support fault investigation when multiple lighting assets within the same feeder experience abnormal conditions. Missing feeder information should not prevent an asset from being managed or monitored by the system.

- **Capture Consistency:** Night surveys should use a consistent smartphone mounting position and camera angle, resolution, frame rate, and locked exposure settings, together with a consistent light-sensor placement, driving lane, travel direction, and vehicle speed. Relevant capture parameters should be recorded for each survey session so that per-pole light measurements remain comparable across repeated surveys of the same pole.

- **Affordability:** The system should remain suitable for rural deployment by using smartphone-based periodic surveys, a low-cost BH1750FVI light-sensing module, and a limited number of IoT monitoring devices rather than requiring dedicated monitoring equipment on every lighting pole.

- **Tolerance of Intermittent Connectivity:** IoT devices must support temporary buffering of telemetry when network connectivity is unavailable, while the Mobile Application must support offline survey data collection and later synchronization when connectivity becomes available.

- **Processing Turnaround:** Submitted survey videos should be processed within a practical time period so that fixture condition results can be reviewed and used for inspection and maintenance planning without significant delay.

- **Recognition and Classification Accuracy:** The ON/OFF recognition model must be evaluated for each class using precision, recall, and F1-score. The resulting Normal / Dim / Out fixture conditions must be evaluated against field verification, and the repeatability of per-pole light measurements across repeated passes must be reported.

- **Field Usability:** GIS maps, fixture condition results, fault management, and work-order interfaces must be understandable and operable by commune personnel without requiring specialized knowledge of GIS or machine learning.

- **Data Retention and Auditability:** Survey imagery, sensor measurements, IoT telemetry, fixture condition results, fault records, and maintenance activities must be retained according to the system's data-retention policy, with an audit trail of important automated findings and user actions.

**e) Theory & Practical:**

**Theory**

The project applies concepts from geographic information systems, low-light computer vision, photometry and asset condition monitoring.

- **GIS Asset and Network Data Models:** Representation of lighting poles, electrical cabinets, road segments, and electrical feeders as spatial entities with their relationships and topology, together with spatial indexing for map queries, asset management, and fault investigation.

- **Photometry and Measurement Geometry:** Principles of illuminance and luminance, the inverse-square and cosine laws, and applicable Vietnamese road lighting requirements. These explain how the illuminance measured at the survey vehicle depends on pole height, lateral distance, and lane position, and why per-pole measurements are compared relative to the same pole under a consistent survey protocol rather than between different poles.

- **Night-time Fixture Detection and ON/OFF Classification:** Deep learning object detection and classification techniques for recognizing lighting fixtures in night imagery and determining whether they are ON or OFF, with consideration of glare, bloom, motion blur, and competing light sources such as vehicle headlights and roadside lights.

- **Mobile Camera Exposure and Image Preprocessing:** Understanding smartphone camera exposure characteristics, including exposure time, ISO/sensor gain, and automatic exposure, and how locking exposure prevents directly imaged fixtures from saturating, together with image preprocessing techniques for improving the consistency of extracted video frames.

- **Spatial and Temporal Data Association:** Techniques for time synchronization between the smartphone and the light-sensing module, peak detection in illuminance time series, and association of video frames, GPS locations, and light measurements with known pole positions in the GIS database.

- **Baseline-based Degradation Detection:** Comparing each pole's light measurement against its own historical baseline across repeated surveys to identify fixtures whose light output has decreased while they still appear ON, including how a provisional baseline is established when only a small number of surveys are available.

- **Electrical Asset Condition Monitoring:** Analysis of electrical measurements and operating information, such as current consumption and operating state, to identify abnormal behavior in monitored lighting assets. Feeder relationships are also considered to support investigation of possible shared electrical causes when multiple lighting assets experience abnormal conditions.

**3.3. Research Information**

**a. Research Problem / Research Question**

Conventional street-lighting inspection commonly focuses on identifying visibly failed or non-operating lighting assets, while a fixture may still turn on but emit significantly less light than expected. The project therefore investigates whether night video captured by a smartphone mounted on a moving vehicle, with the camera angled toward the roadside fixtures, can reliably determine whether each fixture is ON or OFF, and whether per-pole illuminance peaks recorded by a low-cost BH1750FVI light sensor on the same vehicle, compared against each pole's own baseline across repeated surveys, can identify dimmed fixtures that still appear ON. The study also investigates how accurately survey observations can be associated with individual poles using smartphone GPS, timestamps, and registered pole positions, how repeatable per-pole light measurements are across passes under a consistent survey protocol, and how capture conditions such as glare, motion blur, and competing light sources affect ON/OFF recognition. A secondary research focus is whether electrical operating information, such as power state and current consumption from a limited number of IoT-monitored lighting assets, together with feeder relationships represented in the GIS, can provide useful supporting evidence for identifying abnormal electrical behavior and investigating possible shared electrical problems affecting multiple lighting assets.

**b. Research Objectives**

- Construct an annotated night fixture dataset from smartphone video captured on pilot road sections, with frames around each pole passing labelled ON or OFF and associated with pole identifiers, GPS locations, timestamps, and synchronized per-pole light measurements.

- Develop and evaluate a computer vision model for detecting roadside lighting fixtures in night survey imagery and classifying them as ON or OFF, reporting precision, recall, and F1-score for each class.

- Evaluate whether per-pole light measurements compared against each pole's own baseline can identify Dim fixtures, and measure the repeatability of these measurements across repeated passes under the survey protocol.

- Evaluate the accuracy of associating survey observations with individual GIS poles using smartphone GPS, timestamps, illuminance peaks, and registered pole positions, including roads with poles on both sides and intersections.

- Evaluate the effect of practical capture conditions, including glare, bloom, motion blur, and competing light sources such as vehicle headlights, on ON/OFF recognition, and whether locked exposure and a consistent survey protocol improve reliability.

- Evaluate whether electrical operating information, particularly power state and current consumption from selected IoT-monitored lighting assets, can provide useful indicators of abnormal electrical operating conditions.

\- Examine whether feeder relationships represented in the GIS can provide useful contextual information when investigating abnormal conditions affecting multiple lighting assets within the same electrical supply group.

\- Assess the operational feasibility of the proposed approach by considering survey effort, processing time, data synchronization, and field review requirements compared with conventional manual inspection.

**c. Research Scope & Methodology**

The study adopts a quantitative field-based approach on selected rural road sections within a pilot area. The scope focuses on the condition of individual roadside fixtures observable through vehicle-based night surveys, selected grid-connected lighting assets represented in the GIS, and a limited IoT prototype for electrical condition monitoring.

Night surveys are conducted using a smartphone mounted on a moving vehicle with its rear camera angled obliquely upward toward the roadside fixtures, so that a single fixture is captured clearly as the vehicle passes its pole. The smartphone records continuous video together with GPS locations and timestamps, while a BH1750FVI light-sensing module mounted facing upward on the vehicle records timestamped illuminance readings. A consistent survey protocol is applied, including route, driving lane and direction, vehicle speed, smartphone mounting and camera angle, locked exposure settings, and light-sensor placement.

Illuminance peaks in the light-sensor time series and the corresponding video frames are used to identify each pole passing. Each passing is associated with a registered GIS pole using the smartphone GPS track, timestamps, and known pole positions. The frames around each passing are used to construct an annotated dataset of fixtures labelled ON or OFF, together with the pole's peak light measurement.

A computer vision model is trained and evaluated to detect fixtures and classify them as ON or OFF, using precision, recall, and F1-score. Fixture conditions are then derived: OFF fixtures are classified as Out, and ON fixtures whose light measurement falls below a configurable proportion of the pole's baseline are classified as Dim. Derived conditions are compared with field verification, and repeated passes over the same poles are used to measure the repeatability of per-pole light measurements.

The influence of practical capture conditions, including glare and bloom, motion blur, and competing light sources such as vehicle headlights and roadside lights, on ON/OFF recognition is examined using survey data collected under representative field conditions. The study evaluates whether locked exposure, a consistent capture protocol, and image preprocessing improve reliability.

Separately, an IoT electrical monitoring prototype is implemented on selected lighting equipment to collect operating information such as power state and current consumption. Predefined rules are used to identify basic abnormal electrical conditions. Lighting poles and their feeder relationships, where known, are represented in the GIS to provide contextual information when multiple assets associated with the same electrical supply group exhibit abnormal conditions.

The study is limited to a pilot deployment conducted within the project period. Therefore, long-term seasonal variation, equipment ageing, and differences across large numbers of road-lighting technologies or geographic regions are outside the primary scope. Light-sensor measurements are used for relative comparison of the same pole across repeated surveys and are not treated as certified absolute illuminance measurements. Because a per-pole baseline requires repeated surveys, the evaluation of Dim classification depends on the number of surveys that can be completed within the project period.

**d. Expected Scientific Contribution**

An empirical evaluation of whether vehicle-mounted smartphone video can reliably determine the ON/OFF status of individual roadside fixtures, and whether per-pole illuminance peaks from a low-cost BH1750FVI light sensor, compared against each pole's own baseline, can detect dimmed fixtures that an on/off inspection would classify as working. The study also evaluates pole-level association accuracy and the repeatability of low-cost per-pole light measurements under a consistent survey protocol.

Alongside this, the project provides an empirical evaluation of whether sparse electrical IoT monitoring, using information such as power state and current consumption, can provide useful evidence of abnormal operating conditions in selected lighting assets. Feeder relationships represented in the GIS provide additional context for investigating possible shared electrical problems when multiple related lighting assets exhibit abnormal conditions.

The project also produces an annotated rural night fixture dataset containing smartphone night imagery, pole identifiers, GPS locations, timestamps, ON/OFF labels, and synchronized per-pole light measurements. Finally, it demonstrates a reference architecture that combines GIS-based lighting asset and feeder management, periodic vehicle-based night surveys, computer vision ON/OFF recognition, low-cost per-pole light sensing, and sparse electrical IoT monitoring for rural road-lighting management under limited deployment budgets.

**4. Other comments (propose all relative things if have):**

- **The sparse-IoT plus smartphone-survey architecture is intentional:** The project does not require a dedicated IoT monitoring node on every lighting pole. Periodic smartphone-based night surveys provide scalable coverage of individual fixture conditions, while a limited number of IoT devices provide electrical operating information for selected lighting assets. This architecture is intended to reduce deployment cost while still supporting both fixture condition assessment and electrical condition monitoring.

**- Computer vision decides ON/OFF; the light sensor decides brightness:** A camera aimed at a lit fixture tends to saturate, so image brightness alone is not a reliable indicator of whether a fixture has dimmed. The computer vision component therefore determines only whether each fixture is ON or OFF and confirms that the measured light peak comes from that fixture rather than from vehicle headlights or other roadside lights, while the BH1750FVI light sensor provides the brightness information used to identify Dim fixtures. The lighting status of road segments is derived from the condition of the poles along them.

**- Feeder topology provides supporting context for fault investigation:** Lighting poles are associated with their corresponding feeder or electrical supply group where this information is available. Feeder relationships help managers and field engineers investigate whether abnormal conditions affecting several lighting assets may have a shared electrical cause. Feeder information may be obtained from existing records or entered and verified manually, and incomplete feeder information does not prevent other LuxMap functions from operating.

**- Light-sensor measurements provide reference data rather than automatic compliance certification:** A low-cost BH1750FVI light sensor mounted on the survey vehicle records illuminance at the vehicle, which depends on pole height, lateral distance, and lane position as well as fixture output. Measurements are therefore compared only for the same pole across repeated surveys under the same protocol, not between different poles, and the project does not claim that they constitute certified absolute illuminance measurements or formal verification of compliance with road-lighting standards.

**- Consistent survey conditions are important for reliable assessment:** Smartphone imagery can be affected by automatic exposure, vehicle motion, glare, shadows, weather, and surrounding light sources. The project therefore applies a consistent survey protocol covering smartphone mounting position and camera angle, locked exposure settings, driving lane, direction and speed, route planning, GPS and timestamp recording, light-sensor placement, and clock synchronization. Image preprocessing is also investigated to reduce unnecessary variation in captured frames.

**- GIS asset data may come from multiple sources:** Lighting poles, fixtures, electrical cabinets, feeders, and road segments may be imported from existing records when available or entered and updated manually by authorized users. The project does not assume that complete or accurate digital asset records already exist in the pilot area.

**- Field validation is an important project dependency:** The project requires suitable night-survey locations and a sufficient number of fixtures in different conditions (ON, OFF, and visibly dimmed), surveyed repeatedly, for dataset collection and evaluation. Field access, survey safety, weather, traffic, and the availability of representative lighting conditions may affect data collection. The project should therefore allow initial software development and model experimentation to proceed independently of full field deployment where possible.
